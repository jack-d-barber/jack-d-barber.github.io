With antimicrobial resistance, if anyone is left behind, we’re all two steps back.

I’m an interdisciplinary researcher combining quantitative microbiology and critical theory. My work aims to unravel how the political, economic and social structures underpinning our society drive the unequal impacts of the antimicrobial resistance crisis.

I’m currently in the final year of my PhD at the University of Aberdeen in Scotland. Over the last four years, I’ve leveraged my background in quantitative medical microbiology and my experience as a decolonial activist to interrogate existing approaches to mitigating antimicrobial resistance. By disrupting disciplinary boundaries, I’m bridging the gap between microbiology in the lab and its real-world applications, ensuring my research translates into health solutions that are grounded in equity and justice. This translational approach is crucial in driving microbiological research that moves beyond academic circles and produces material societal benefits.
